The mission

SpaceAero documents a multi-year journey into model and high-power rocketry — from first kit builds to a scratch-designed vehicle carrying its own flight computer.

Every flight is a chance to measure something: altitude, acceleration, stability, recovery. The goal isn't just to launch, but to record the data, learn what it says, and share the whole process — successes and failures alike — so other students can skip the mistakes I make first.

The roadmap

Year 1 — Build & measure

A series of model rockets with onboard data logging, working toward NAR Level 1 and Level 2 certification flights.

Year 2 — Go bigger

One larger project: a scratch build with a recoverable payload, or a glider/drone platform for aerial data.

Compete

Targeting The American Rocketry Challenge (TARC) and NASA TechRise, flying with the local CENJARS club (NAR #698).

Log 01 — My first instrumented rocket flight

An Estes Alpha III with an onboard sensor board on an A8-3 motor. OpenRocket predicted 312 ft; the real flight reached 287 ft (–8%), with clean, usable sensor data.
