FACT SHEET (checked Nov 14, 2026 — use your own words, don't copy sentences)
- Flight 15 launched from Starbase, Texas. Ship 42 on top of Super Heavy Booster 22.
- Ship spent about 22.5 hours in orbit (~14 orbits, ~260 km / 161 mi up) — the longest Starship mission so far.
- Previous record: Flight 14 in September 2026, the first time Starship reached orbit (~3 hours).
- Main headline: on Nov 14 the ship flew back to the launch tower and was caught by the "chopsticks" arms — the first time ever for the upper stage.
- During reentry, four flaps steer the ship to adjust its path.
- The booster catch was ABORTED (SpaceX said a guidance issue). Booster catches had worked three times before, last in March 2025.
- SpaceX commentator Dan Huot called the ship catch "the holy grail of spaceflight."
Sources: Aviation Week, Space.com, CNN live updates, Reuters (Nov 14, 2026)
